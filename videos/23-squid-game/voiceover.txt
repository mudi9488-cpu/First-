In this game, 456 people play children's games. The winner gets 45.6 billion won. But if you lose, you're out. Forever.

My name is Gi-hun. Player 456. I was broke, divorced, and I owed money to very bad people.

One day at the subway, a man in a suit challenged me to a game. Every time I lost, he slapped me. Every time I won, he paid me. Then he asked: Want to make some real money? And he gave me a card. Circle. Triangle. Square.

I woke up on a secret island. 456 people in green tracksuits. All of them broke. All of them desperate. And guards in pink, with masks.

I wasn't alone. There was Sang-woo, my childhood friend, the smartest kid in our neighborhood. Sae-byeok, a pickpocket who never smiled. And the old man, Player 001, who just wanted to have fun.

Game one. Red Light, Green Light. A giant doll. When she turns around, you freeze. If you move, you're out. Half of us never made it to the finish line.

Above our beds hung a giant golden piggy bank. Every player who was out added 100 million won.

We voted to go home. And we did. But life outside was even worse. So almost everyone came back.

Game two. Honeycomb candy. Cut out a shape without breaking it. I got the umbrella. The hardest one. So I licked it.

Game three. Tug of war. Ten against ten, on two towers. Our team was weak. But the old man, Player 001, knew a trick. Three steps forward. Then pull.

Game four. Marbles. Teams of two. I played against the old man. He forgot things. And I cheated. But he gave me his last marble anyway, and smiled. We're friends, he said. Friends share everything.

Game five. A glass bridge. Two panels every step. One holds you. One breaks. The man in front of me could tell the glass apart. Until the guards turned off the lights. Sixteen players. Only three made it across.

The final game. Just me and my childhood friend, Sang-woo. In the rain. On a giant squid drawn in the sand. I asked him to stop. To go home together. He didn't.

I won. 45.6 billion won. And I couldn't spend a single coin. When I got home, my mom was gone.

A year later, I got another card. In a hospital bed was the old man. Player 001. Alive. He wasn't a player. He created the game. Because he was bored. Watching was fun, he said. But playing was more fun.

So I dyed my hair red, turned around at the airport, and walked straight back into the game. Because someone had to end it.
