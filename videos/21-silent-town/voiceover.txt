In this town, nobody is allowed to make a sound after sunset. Not a word. Not a sneeze. Because something in the forest is listening.

Dogs wear muzzles at night. Phones are always on silent. Kids get grounded for burping.

Last winter, Mr. Miller sneezed once. The next morning, his house was covered in giant claw marks.

The mayor says the silence keeps us safe. And in return, our town gets free electricity. Every light. Every house. For free.

My name is Max. I'm thirteen, and I've followed the rules my whole life.

Until one night, I heard something. Not a growl. Not a roar. Crying.

It came from the old factory behind the town hall. The one with the sign Keep out.

I knew the rule. But it sounded so sad. So I sneaked out. Barefoot. Holding my breath.

Inside, there were wires all over the walls. And they all led to one cage.

Inside the cage was a baby monster. Fluffy. Big eyes. No bigger than my dog.

And every time it cried, its tears glowed. A machine collected them and sent them through the wires. Straight into our town.

That's when I got it. Our free electricity was a baby's tears.

And the monster in the forest wasn't hunting us. She was looking for her kid. The silence was never to keep us safe. It was so she could never hear it.

On the cage was a sign. Property of the mayor. Do not open.

So I opened it.

The baby crawled into my arms, looked at me, and sneezed. Loud.

The whole town went silent. Then, from the forest, footsteps. Huge footsteps. Coming closer.

Lights turned on in every house. People ran outside. The mayor screamed: Catch that kid!

But it was too late. She was already here. Taller than the town hall. Eyes glowing like headlights.

Everyone froze. Except me. I walked right up to her, and held out her baby.

She looked at me for a really long time. Then the baby jumped into her arms, and she made a sound I'll never forget. She purred.

Then she turned to the mayor, and roared. He flew backwards straight into a trash can.

And one by one, every light in town went out.

We lost our free electricity. But we got something better. We can talk at night again.

And every full moon, a giant fluffy monster waves at me from the forest. Oh, and the mayor? He's the only one in town who still whispers after sunset.
