In this town, everyone opens one treasure chest every single day. And everyone is hunting for one thing. The Pirate Token.

Find it, and the whole shop is yours. For free. For fourteen days.

Every morning at eight, the chest hall opens. You walk in, pick one chest, and that's it. One chance. Until tomorrow.

But the chance is 1 in 137,500. Most people open chests their whole life and never see it.

And there is one rule. The token can't be sold. It can't be gifted. It belongs to whoever opens the chest. Forever.

People still tried. The rich guy across the street offered $10,000 just for an account that had it.

His name is Mr. Vance. He owns half the town. But not the token.

My name is Leo. I'm thirteen, and I have opened 412 chests. Wood. Wood. A bandage. Wood.

My little sister Mia laughs at me every morning. She says the token isn't real.

Last year, a girl two streets away supposedly found one. The next day, her whole family moved away. Nobody knows where.

Then the mayor appeared on every screen in town. Treasure Hour. For one hour, the chance goes up 1.5 times.

The whole town went crazy. People lined up in front of the chest hall at four in the morning.

Mr. Vance even paid people to open chests for him. Hundreds of them.

I was there at three.

When the doors opened, hundreds of chests were glowing. Everyone opened at the same time. Empty. Empty. Empty. Somebody cried. Somebody kicked a chest.

Then it was my turn. I touched the lid, and it was warm. Way too warm.

I opened it, and the whole hall went silent.

Inside was a coin. Black and gold, with a skull on it.

The Pirate Token.

My hands were shaking. Mia was right behind me. Her mouth was wide open.

My screen lit up. Shop unlocked. Fourteen days.

Everyone was staring at me. Then the rich guy pushed through the crowd.

He didn't offer money this time. He just whispered: You don't know what that token really does.

Then he looked at Mia. And smiled.

That night, someone knocked on our door. Three times. Mia ran into my room. Leo, she whispered, that's the same knock the girl's family heard. And when I looked at the token, it was counting down.
