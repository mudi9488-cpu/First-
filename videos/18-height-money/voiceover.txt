In this town, your height shows how much money you have. Every dollar in your bank account makes you taller.

Got ten dollars? You're as tall as a cat. A thousand dollars? Normal size. A million dollars? You're taller than a house.

The richest man in town, Mr. Gold, is so tall that birds build nests in his hair.

Babies are born the size of a peanut. And grandparents who saved money their whole life have to watch out for airplanes.

Rich kids look down on everyone. Literally. When you buy something, you shrink right in the middle of the store. And when someone wins the lottery, they shoot up so fast they crash through the roof. Banks don't even need cards here. They just measure you.

So everybody wants to be tall. Nobody wants to be small. Small means poor.

My name is Sam. I'm fifteen, and I'm exactly four inches tall. Because my family has nine dollars and fifty cents.

The stairs at school are like mountains. My classmates almost step on me every single day. And my lunch is one single crumb. It lasts three days.

I tried everything to get taller. I sold lemonade. Twenty cents. I grew one millimeter. I found a coin on the street, and a pigeon almost carried me away.

Then the mayor announced the Giant Games. One hundred thousand people in the stadium. The winner gets ten million dollars.

Round one was basketball. The giants just dropped the ball into the hoop. Round two was tug of war. I got stepped on twice. But I didn't give up.

The final challenge: find the golden key and open the safe. Whoever opens it keeps the money.

The giants tore the stadium apart. They lifted cars. They flipped benches. Mr. Gold even looked above the clouds.

But I saw something they couldn't. A tiny shine, deep inside a crack in the ground.

The key was way too small for any giant to grab. But not for me.

Then Mr. Gold saw me. He reached down with his giant hand. I jumped into the crack just in time. His fingers were too big.

I climbed down, grabbed it, and walked straight to the safe. The whole stadium went silent. Click.

The screen went crazy. One thousand. One million. Ten million.

And I started growing. And growing. And growing. For the first time in my life, I could see the whole town. Kids who laughed at me suddenly had to look up.

And Mr. Gold? He had bet all his money that I would lose. He shrank so fast, he fell right into the crack.

So I gave him my old lunch. One single crumb. Trust me, it lasts three days.
