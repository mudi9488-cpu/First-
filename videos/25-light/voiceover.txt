In this town, a light appears above your head whenever someone is thinking about you.

One person thinking about you? A tiny spark. Your whole family? A warm little lamp. The popular kids at my school glow like streetlights. And the mayor glows so bright, you can't even look at him. And when nobody thinks about you? Nothing. Just darkness.

My name is Ben. I'm thirteen. And my light has never turned on. Not once.

Not on my birthday. Not when I won the science fair. Not even when I broke my arm. At school, they call me Lightless. Every night, I'd stare at the dark above my head. Hoping. Just once.

I live with my uncle. My mom left when I was three. That's all he ever told me. He never talks about her. He just looks away.

Then one night, at 3:12 a.m., my room turned white.

I looked up. There was a light above my head. Not a spark. Not a lamp. The brightest light this town had ever seen.

The neighbors ran outside in their pajamas. Cars stopped in the middle of the street. Somebody called the news. Even the mayor's light looked tiny next to mine.

And it didn't turn off. Not that night. Not the next morning. At school, nobody called me Lightless anymore. Even Jake, the most popular kid in school, just stared. Kids took pictures. Girls who never talked to me suddenly said hi.

But something was strange. The light was pulsing. Slowly. Like a heartbeat. And a thin beam was shooting out of it. Pointing north. Like it wanted me to follow.

So that night, I followed it. Past the school. Through the forest. Up the hill. To the old hospital nobody visits anymore. The beam got brighter with every step.

The beam went through a door on the top floor. Room 312. The light above my head was beating faster now.

Inside was a woman. Thin. Pale. Surrounded by machines. Her eyes were open. She looked at me, and whispered my name. The heart monitor next to her was beeping in the same rhythm as my light.

It was my mom. She never left. She'd been in a coma. For ten years. Ten years in the dark. Just like me. And that first night, at 3:12 a.m., she woke up. And the very first thing she thought about was me.

My uncle cried when he saw us. He'd visited her every Sunday. He just never knew how to tell me.

Now my light never turns off. Not even for a second. But this morning, a second light appeared above my head. And this one is red.
