In this town, every time you lie, a red light turns on above your head. Last Monday, everyone's light turned on. Except mine. And the mayor's.

Now the mayor was standing at our front door. Smiling.

Noah, he said, the town needs its most honest kid. Come with me to the TV studio. Tell everyone the lights are real. They will believe you.

My mom grabbed my arm. Her light was blinking. But she didn't say a word.

So I got in the black car. The whole town was watching from behind their curtains. Hundreds of red lights in the dark windows.

The studio was huge. Cameras everywhere. A giant sign said LIVE. The mayor gave me a card. It said: The lights never lie.

Then he whispered: Just read this, and you get a golden medal for the rest of your life.

Even the cameraman had a red light. He looked at me and gave me a tiny nod.

But while he was talking to the cameraman, I saw a door. Staff only. And behind it, something was humming.

I opened it. Inside was a giant machine. Thousands of buttons. And every button had a name on it.

My teacher. The baker. My mom. Everyone's button was red. Then I found one that said Mayor. It was switched off.

That's when I understood. The lights never showed lies. The mayor controlled them. Anyone who found out got a red light, so nobody would believe them.

On Monday, his speech had a glitch. Everyone heard him talking about the machine. Everyone except me. I was asleep.

And right there, above my head, something turned on. My light. Because now I knew the truth too.

The mayor saw it. His face went white. But the red light on the camera was already on. We were live.

So I didn't read the card. I looked straight into the camera and said: The lights are fake. The mayor controls them. And I have never lied in my life.

And everyone knew that was true.

All over town, people stepped out of their houses. One by one. Red lights everywhere. And nobody was afraid anymore.

Then I walked back to the machine and flipped one more switch.

The mayor's light turned on. It was the biggest, reddest light this town has ever seen. You could see it from space.

The next day, we turned the machine off forever. My mom hugged me so hard my light flickered. And me? My light stayed on for exactly 24 hours. Best 24 hours of my life.
