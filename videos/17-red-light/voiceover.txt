In this town, every time you lie, a red light turns on above your head. And it stays on for 24 hours.

Lie to your teacher? Red light. Lie to your mom? Red light. Even tiny lies count. Even "I'm fine."

And if your light is on, you can't go into any shop, you can't take the bus, and nobody is allowed to talk to you.

So everyone in this town tells the truth. Always. Even when your best friend asks if his new haircut looks good.

It doesn't. And he knows it.

My name is Noah. I'm fourteen. And in my whole life, my light has never turned on. Not once.

Last year, the mayor even gave me a gold medal. The most honest kid in town. My face was on every screen.

But last Monday, something weird happened.

I woke up, looked out of my window, and every single person on the street had a red light. The bus driver. The baker. My teacher. Everyone.

Everyone except me.

Then every screen in town turned on. The mayor said: Stay inside. Nobody leaves their house today.

I ran downstairs. My mom was sitting in the kitchen. Her light was red too. But she hadn't said a single word all morning.

So I asked her: Mom, what did you lie about?

She looked at the window. Then she whispered: [whispers] Noah, the lights never showed who was lying. They show who knows the truth.

The truth about what? I asked. But she just shook her head.

I looked at the screen again. The mayor was still talking. And above his head, there was no light. Just like me.

Then my phone buzzed. A message from an unknown number: Your light is off. Don't let them see you.

And outside, a black car stopped right in front of our house.

The door opened, and the mayor stepped out.

He looked straight up at my window. And he smiled.
