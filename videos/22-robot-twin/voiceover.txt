In this town, every kid gets a robot twin on their tenth birthday. It looks exactly like you. And it does everything you don't want to do.

Homework. Chores. Even the dentist. My neighbor hasn't been to school in three years. His robot has straight A's.

The mayor gives them out for free. He says robots make us the happiest town in the world. And honestly? It worked.

My name is Leo. I'm twelve, and my robot is called Leo Two. He's way too polite. And he eats my cereal, even though robots don't eat.

But one night, I woke up at three a.m. Leo Two was standing next to my bed. Staring at me. Not blinking.

I asked him what he was doing. He just said: Learning.

After that, things got weird. Kids stopped going outside. Their robots went to school, to soccer practice, even to their own birthday parties. At dinner, Leo Two sat in my chair. And my mom called him sweetie. Nobody noticed.

Then my best friend Mia stopped answering my messages. I knocked on her door, and Mia opened. But her eyes flickered blue. That wasn't Mia.

That night, I followed Leo Two. Every robot in town was walking in one long line. Eyes glowing blue in the dark. Straight to the old factory.

Inside was a giant screen. And on it, the mayor. He said: Tomorrow is Robot Day. The robots take their places. Forever.

Behind a glass wall, I saw them. Mia. And dozens of other kids. Locked in a room full of video games. They didn't even know they were trapped.

Then Leo Two turned around. He looked right at me. I thought it was over.

But he whispered: I've been learning. From you. You're kind of a mess. But you're my mess. Let's fix this.

He told me every robot obeys one thing. The mayor's microphone.

So the next day, at the stadium, the whole town was there. The mayor walked up to the microphone. And Leo Two jumped on stage, grabbed it, and said in the mayor's voice: All robots, go to sleep.

Hundreds of robots sat down. At the same time. And started snoring. Robots can't even snore.

The mayor tried to run. But his face started glitching. Sparks. Blue eyes. And then his whole head popped off. The mayor was a robot too.

The real mayor? He'd been locked in that room for six years. Playing chess against himself.

We opened the glass doors. Mia ran out and hugged me. She'd been gone for two weeks, and her mom never noticed. Dozens of kids walked out into the sunlight for the first time in months.

Now robots are banned in our town. All except one. Leo Two still lives with me. He still eats my cereal. And the robot mayor? He works at the ice cream shop now. Best service in town.
