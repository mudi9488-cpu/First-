In this town, every mirror has to be covered at midnight. Every single one. Bathrooms. Hallways. Even car mirrors. Because after midnight, your reflection stops copying you. It moves on its own. It smiles when you're sad. And if you stare at it for too long, it remembers your face.

But Mrs. Hale from next door forgot. Just once. The next morning, she looked totally normal. Same smile. Same voice. But she was writing with her left hand. And she'd been right-handed for seventy years. Nobody said anything. In this town, you just don't ask.

My name is Theo. I'm twelve. And for the last week, something has been wrong with my sister Emma. She's fifteen. She's annoying, but she's the best sister in the world.

She stopped calling me Teddy. She forgot our dog's name. And this morning, she buttered her toast with her left hand. When I asked her about it, she just smiled. Like she was practicing.

Then one night, I heard a sound in the hallway. The cloth on the big mirror had fallen down. The clock said 12:01. I knew I shouldn't look. But I did.

My reflection was standing there. Not moving. Not copying me. Just staring. And behind it, in the mirror's hallway, was Emma. The real Emma. Crying. Banging on the glass. But there was no sound.

She breathed on the mirror and wrote with her finger. Backwards. It's not me.

That's when I understood. The girl sleeping in Emma's room wasn't my sister. It was her reflection. And it had switched places with her.

The only way to switch back is at midnight. Both of them have to touch the same mirror at the same time. I wasn't scared of the dark anymore. I was scared of my own sister. So the next night, I took the cover off every mirror in the house.

At 11:59, I woke up the fake Emma. I told her I had a nightmare. I asked her to walk me to the bathroom. She smiled. But only half of her face moved. And her hair was parted on the wrong side.

At exactly midnight, she looked into the mirror. And the real Emma pressed her hand against the glass from the other side. The fake Emma screamed. Every light in the house flickered. The glass rippled like water. And then it pulled her in.

Emma fell into my arms. Warm. Real. Calling me Teddy. The mirror cracked from corner to corner. Inside, the reflection was banging on the glass. Then it was gone.

Our dog finally stopped growling at her. Emma writes with her right hand again. And we still cover every mirror at midnight. But this morning, when I brushed my teeth, my reflection finished before I did.
