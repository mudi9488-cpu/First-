In this town, nobody is allowed to look at the moon. Not even for a second. Because if you look at the moon, it looks back.

Every night at nine, the sirens go off. Windows get covered. Kids sleep with blindfolds. And the streets stay completely empty. The rule is simple. Eyes on the ground. Always. Even the dogs know. They sleep facing the wall.

Last year, Mr. Park forgot. He looked up for just one second. The next morning, his house was empty. Only his glasses on the floor. Still warm. The police searched for weeks. Nobody ever found him.

My name is Noah. I'm twelve. And my little sister Lily has never seen the moon. She's seven. She asks a hundred questions a day. She always asked me what it looks like. I told her: like a giant light bulb. Boring.

But one night, I heard her window open. Lily was on the roof. Blindfold in her hand. Looking straight up. I climbed out after her. Eyes on my shoes.

And the moon opened its eyes. Two huge white eyes. Staring right at her. The whole town went quiet. Even the wind stopped.

Then a beam of light came down. Searching the street. Window by window. House by house. And every time it touched a house, the lights inside went out.

I grabbed Lily and ran. The beam followed us. Every streetlight exploded as it passed. The sirens went off. Police cars everywhere. People screaming behind their curtains.

We hid in the old mirror shop at the end of our street. Hundreds of mirrors. Dark and dusty. Lily was shaking. The beam stopped right outside the door. And that's when I had an idea.

If the moon finds you by looking at you, what happens if it sees itself?

We carried every mirror up to the roof. All of them. On the chimneys. On the antennas. On the edge of the roof. Lily held the last one. The beam came closer. And closer. Then it hit the mirrors.

The light bounced back. Straight into its own eyes. The moon screamed. The whole sky shook. And for the first time in a hundred years, the moon closed its eyes.

The next morning, Mr. Park was sitting on his porch. Confused. Hungry. And alive. And so was everyone else who had ever looked up.

Now we're allowed to look up again. Lily looks at the moon every night. She says it looks like a giant sleeping light bulb.

But last night, I saw a second moon. And this one was already looking at me. So now I need your help. Should I tell Lily?
