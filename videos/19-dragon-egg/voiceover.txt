In this town, every kid gets a dragon on their twelfth birthday. Except me.

Some get fire dragons. Some get ice dragons. One kid even got a dragon that breathes bubbles.

Dragons fly you to school. They carry your groceries. And the richest family in town has a dragon so big it blocks out the sun. Dragons even have their own traffic lights. Red means land. Green means fly.

My name is Kai. I'm twelve, and on my birthday, everyone's egg hatched. Mine didn't.

My best friend got a red dragon that can fly faster than a plane. I got an egg that just sat there. Doing nothing.

And then there was Blake. The richest kid in town. His dragon was called Titan, and it was as big as a mountain. Every day, Blake flew over my house and dropped his ice cream on me. On purpose.

I tried everything. I kept it warm with a hair dryer. I read it bedtime stories. I even sat on it for three days. Nothing. I sang to it. I showed it pictures of other dragons. I even put it in the microwave. Just for a second. Okay, that was a bad idea.

Every morning, my classmates flew to school. I took the stairs. All four hundred of them. Our teacher even gave me a special parking spot. For my backpack.

Then the mayor announced the Great Dragon Race. The winner gets a golden saddle and becomes the hero of the town. Every dragon in town signed up. Blake said I could join if I wanted. As the finish line.

But the night before the race, a giant storm hit the town. Lightning struck the dragon tower. Every single dragon got scared and flew away. Even Titan. The whole town went dark. People hid in their houses. Blake was crying under a table.

Then I heard something. Crack. My egg was glowing.

Out came the smallest dragon I've ever seen. The size of a potato. Everyone laughed. Blake laughed the loudest. He said, nice potato.

But my dragon wasn't scared of the storm. It opened its mouth and ate the lightning. One bolt. Two bolts. Three bolts.

And with every bolt, it grew bigger. And bigger. Until it was bigger than the richest family's dragon.

I climbed on its back, and we flew straight into the storm. We found every lost dragon and brought them home. Even Titan. He was hiding behind a waterfall, shaking like a leaf.

The next morning, I won the race. By about three miles. Blake came in second. Titan was too tired to fly.

Turns out my dragon wasn't late. It was just waiting for the right weather.
